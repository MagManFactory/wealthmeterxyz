The 2026 Retirement Catch-Up Window at Ages 60 to 63

In 2026, workplace-plan participants who turn 60, 61, 62, or 63 may have a higher catch-up contribution limit. The opportunity depends on the plan, the participant's compensation, and, for some workers, the Roth catch-up rule.

Plan eligibility · payroll elections · Roth catch-up · contribution ceiling

The higher catch-up provision created by SECURE 2.0 is now in effect. It is narrow by design: it applies to people who turn ages 60 through 63 during the calendar year and participate in an eligible workplace plan. It does not increase an IRA contribution limit and it does not bypass a plan's own operational rules.

For 2026, the regular employee deferral limit is $24,500. The general age-50-or-older catch-up is $8,000, while the higher age-60-through-63 catch-up is $11,250 for most eligible workplace plans.

Identify the eligible plans and ages

The IRS identifies most 401(k), 403(b), governmental 457(b), and federal Thrift Savings Plan arrangements for the $11,250 higher catch-up. Eligibility is based on the age the participant turns during the calendar year, not the age on the day the payroll election is changed.

SIMPLE plans use different numbers. For 2026, the regular SIMPLE catch-up is $4,000 and the higher age-60-through-63 SIMPLE catch-up is $5,250. An employer plan must permit catch-up contributions before a participant can use them.

Check the Roth catch-up requirement

Beginning in 2026, participants in plans with Roth features that offer catch-up contributions must make catch-up contributions on a Roth basis when prior-year wages from that plan sponsor exceeded $150,000. That wage threshold is indexed; the IRS page states the 2026 amount. The rule concerns wages with the plan sponsor, not household income, net worth, or investment income.

A participant should not assume every part of the annual contribution must be Roth. The catch-up portion is the relevant portion. Plan administration and payroll settings determine how the requirement is implemented, so the plan recordkeeper is the appropriate source for an account-specific answer.

Do not combine limits that belong to different accounts

For 2026, the regular 401(k), 403(b), governmental 457, and TSP employee deferral limit is $24,500. Eligible people 60 through 63 can generally add an $11,250 catch-up, for a potential $35,750 employee deferral total, subject to plan rules and compensation. The figure is not a target and excludes employer contributions and limits that apply elsewhere.

Traditional and Roth IRAs have separate limits. The 2026 IRA contribution limit is $7,500 and the age-50-or-older IRA catch-up is $1,100. An IRA contribution does not consume the workplace-plan catch-up window, but tax treatment and eligibility can be more complicated than a single annual number suggests.

Fit the contribution into the wider plan

A larger payroll deferral changes cash flow and may affect debt reduction, emergency reserves, employer matching, and estimated taxes.
