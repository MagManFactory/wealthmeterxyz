Evidence and method

How Historical Return Windows works

Historical Return Windows compounds each complete run of annual U.S. returns and annualizes the result. Its range describes past outcomes for different starting years; it does not estimate your chance of profit.

By WealthMeter · Prepared with AI assistance · Sources and method reviewed 14 October 2026

What is calculated?

For a window of N years, multiply (1 + annual return) for every year, then take the Nth root and subtract one. This geometric annualized return differs from the arithmetic average. For example, +20% followed by −20% leaves 96% of the starting balance: an annualized loss of about 2.02%, even though the arithmetic average is zero.

What does the replay add?

The replay uses the selected stock, bond and bill weights with annual rebalancing. The monthly contribution input is modeled as an inflation-indexed annual contribution using an approximate within-year return of half the annual portfolio return. These timing assumptions can materially change results. The data retain stock total returns, a 10-year Treasury bond series and a bill-rate proxy; the three are not interchangeable investable products.

Limitations

One U.S. historical dataset cannot establish future returns. Fees, taxes, personal inflation, intra-year cash flows and trading frictions are not a complete part of this replay. A median historical result is not a forecast.
