AUTOMATION IS A CHOICE, NOT A COEFFICIENT

Aging is measurable.
Technology’s payoff is conditional.

AI exposure describes tasks that could interact with AI. It does not tell us how much firms will adopt, how many jobs will disappear, or how quickly productivity will grow.

Factory robot density concerns manufacturing employees. Connectivity concerns access and use. Neither establishes a whole-economy automation dividend. The productivity slider lets you test an assumption without turning these indicators into a prediction.

SOURCES & METHOD

The assumptions behind the picture.

Population: one release, one consistent definition

United Nations, Department of Economic and Social Affairs, Population Division (2024). World Population Prospects 2024, Online Edition, POP/DB/WPP/Rev.2024/POP/F07-1, both sexes. Annual estimates end in 2023; projections begin in 2024. Charts use 2026–2050 medium projections, which are uncertain rather than locked in. We found WPP 2024 in the official download catalog checked 15 October 2026; we do not splice editions.

The old-age dependency ratio is 100 × population aged 65+ ÷ population aged 15–64. Working-age share (%) = 10000 ÷ (100 + total dependency ratio). The publisher rounds ratios to three decimals, so the derived share is approximate. Display values are rounded; calculations retain source precision. No synthetic age cohorts or population totals are inferred.

UN download center · Download the extracted data · CC BY 3.0 IGO. Derived by WealthMeter; no UN endorsement.

Scenario: exact multiplication, explicit assumptions

Output per person = output per employed person × (employed people ÷ population aged 15–64) × (population aged 15–64 ÷ total population). This is an exact accounting identity. Productivity per employed person includes hours worked; hours are not modelled separately.

Let W = working-age share in 2050 ÷ its 2026 share, E = 1 + total employment-intensity change / 100, and g = annual output-per-person goal as a decimal. Required annual productivity growth = (1 + g) ÷ (W × E)1/24 − 1. At year t after 2026, scenario index = 100 × (1 + p)t × Et/24 × working-age share(t) ÷ share(2026). Goal index = 100 × (1 + g)t.

Employment intensity is a relative factor, not percentage points and not a verified national employment rate. Its ±20% range is a sensitivity exercise, not a claim that every setting is feasible. Default 1% productivity and 1% goal are illustrative choices. Automation, investment, skills and organization could influence productivity, but the tool makes no deterministic conversion from AI exposure to growth. The same settings apply to both economies.

Evidence limits and changes from the original

The supplied prototype’s productivity history, labour participation, older-worker rates, AI shares, robot counts and connectivity scores lacked row-level release and year evidence. We omitted these numerical series rather than label them verified. No historical productivity CAGR is shown. WDI GDP per employed person measures labour productivity, not total factor productivity.

The arbitrary technology composite, typology partitions, retirement-cohort rule and additive growth bridge were removed. No restricted IFR or ITU tables are republished. These context indicators do not feed the model. Missing values are not treated as zero. Country coverage is the original 20 economies; it is not a representative sample or a ranking of policy quality.
