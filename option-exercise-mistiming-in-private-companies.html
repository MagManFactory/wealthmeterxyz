LONGFORM · COMPENSATION AND HOUSEHOLD EXPOSURE

Option Exercise Mistiming in Private Companies

OCTOBER 21, 2026 · WEALTHMETER

An employee has vested options in a private company and a deadline approaching. Exercising buys shares at the stated price, but the shares may remain unsellable. A decision made to preserve an apparent gain can consume cash and create tax exposure before any cash return exists.

An option is a right to buy shares under an award agreement. The grant and plan documents govern vesting, exercise price, expiration, and what happens after employment ends. The company's current share valuation may be relevant to tax calculations, but it is not a firm offer to buy the employee's shares. The Securities and Exchange Commission defines liquidity risk as the risk of being unable to find a market when a holder wants to sell.

First identify the option type

The Internal Revenue Service (IRS) distinguishes incentive stock options (ISOs), which are statutory options, from nonstatutory stock options (NSOs). An ISO generally does not create regular gross income at exercise, yet the spread between exercise price and fair market value may affect alternative minimum tax. The final result depends on the taxpayer's full return and later disposition. For a typical NSO without a readily determinable value at grant, exercise generally creates compensation income equal to the fair market value of the stock received less the price paid. Restricted shares and unusual award terms can change the timing.

That distinction makes a generic exercise calculator inadequate. The person needs the award type, vested share count, exercise price, relevant fair market value, grant and exercise dates, and the plan's actual deadlines. IRS Form 3921 records key ISO exercise facts. It does not make the stock liquid or settle the household's tax bill.

Three clocks can point in different directions

The first clock is contractual: the option expires on the date in the agreement or under the applicable post-employment terms. The second is tax: exercise and later sale can fall in different tax years, with different treatment for ISO and NSO shares. The third is liquidity: the company may never offer a tender, permit a secondary sale, or reach a public market within the employee's planning horizon. The household bears cash and concentration risk while waiting.

Exercising early can reduce a future spread if the company's value rises, but that forecast is uncertain and the company could lose value. Waiting can preserve household cash and optionality, but it can also leave a larger spread or let the contractual window close. Neither choice is inherently correct. The decision depends on the documents, the tax analysis, available reserves, and the value of keeping cash outside a single private company.

Model cash before paper gain

Calculate the exercise payment from vested shares and the strike price. Separately estimate federal, state, and payroll tax effects with a qualified tax professional; avoid treating the current valuation as sale proceeds. Test whether the household can fund both the exercise and a plausible tax payment while maintaining its reserve for living costs and emergencies. If the plan requires quick action after departure, evaluate that constraint before resignation or termination when possible.

Stress the downside explicitly. Private-company shares may fall in value, remain restricted, be diluted, or lack a buyer. A future financing round or company valuation can differ from the value of an employee's common shares after preferences and transfer limits. Money committed to exercise cannot cover a mortgage or tax bill unless the stock is actually sold. Borrowing to exercise adds a repayment obligation to an uncertain asset.

Keep records that survive the transaction

Save the grant agreement, plan, exercise confirmation, valuation used for tax, payroll records, and any Form 3921. Later sale reporting needs a correct basis and holding-period history. The IRS notes that broker-reported basis for option shares may omit amounts already included in wages, so records should be reconciled before filing. A later sale can also change ISO treatment if holding-period requirements are not met.

The actionable question is whether the household can bear the cost of owning these shares if no sale arrives soon. A tax estimate and the signed award terms can clarify that question; a portal gain cannot.
