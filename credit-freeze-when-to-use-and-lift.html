A Credit Freeze Is Free: When to Use One and How to Lift It

A security freeze limits access to a credit report for new-credit decisions. It is free, does not change a credit score, and can be lifted when a lender needs to check a file.

credit reports · identity theft · security freezes · fraud alerts

A freeze is a preventive control, not an identity-recovery service. It can make it harder for someone to open a new credit account in another person’s name, but it does not erase fraudulent accounts, prevent every form of identity theft, or block all access to a file.

To freeze nationwide credit files, contact Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion separately. A freeze is free by law. CFPB states that a request made by secure electronic means or phone must be placed within one business day, while a requested lift must be completed within one hour.

Decide when a freeze fits

A freeze is sensible for people who are not actively applying for credit and want a standing barrier against new-account fraud. It can also be placed after suspected exposure of sensitive information, but there is no requirement to wait for a breach. A parent or guardian can request a freeze for an eligible protected consumer.

Plan before a credit application

A lender, landlord, insurer, or employer may need access to a report. The federal freeze requirement does not apply to employment, tenant-screening, or insurance requests. Before an application, ask which bureau will be used and whether the organization needs a temporary lift or a permanent removal. Lift only the necessary file where possible, then confirm that the freeze is restored when the check is complete.

Existing creditors, certain government agencies, and credit-monitoring firms a consumer has hired may still access a frozen file. You can also obtain and review your own reports while a freeze is in place.

Do not confuse freezes, locks, and alerts

Credit locks are commercial products and may carry a fee; the CFPB says they are no more effective than free security freezes. A fraud alert asks creditors to verify identity before granting new credit but does not prevent them from seeing the report. An initial fraud alert can be placed by contacting one bureau, which must notify the other two. A freeze must be requested at all three bureaus separately.

Security freezeLimits prospective creditors’ access to the file.Place and lift separately at all three nationwide bureaus.
Fraud alertRequests identity verification before new credit.One contacted bureau notifies the other two.

Make it part of routine financial control

Check credit reports for unfamiliar accounts even with a freeze in place. The emergency-fund framework addresses cash resilience after a disruption, while the deductible analysis shows why a household needs a plan before a claim or credit event forces fast decisions.
