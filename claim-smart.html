Methods, assumptions & official sources

Reviewed October 15, 2026. This tool compares an individual worker’s retirement benefits for birth cohorts 1943 and later. It does not determine eligibility: SSA normally requires sufficient covered work (typically 40 credits). The FRA input uses cohort rules, not a fixed age 67.

For each month before FRA, benefits are reduced by 5/9 of 1% for the first 36 months and 5/12 of 1% for additional months. After FRA, delayed credits add 2/3 of 1% of the FRA benefit per month for these cohorts, stopping at 70. The automatic estimate holds the earnings record fixed, unlike SSA estimates that may assume further work. Separate SSA estimates can replace this approximation. We retain precision internally and round only displayed dollars; SSA has its own rounding rules.

Monthly planning approximation: the selected age represents an eligible entitlement month. The first deposit arrives one model month later, after interest on the opening balance. Actual payment days, birthday eligibility (including first/second-of-month rules), and calendar effects are not modeled. All delayed credits are included immediately for comparison; for starts before 70, SSA may apply some credits the following January. The result is not an exact payment quote.

Common-dollar benefits receive one common annual COLA index beginning at the reference age, before and after claiming, with annual steps at reference-age anniversaries. SSA uses calendar COLAs after eligibility; this simplified index translates today's-dollar estimates into nominal scenarios and is not a forecast of calendar SSA increases. Claim-time future-dollar estimates are divided by that same index at their claim month, preventing inflation already embedded in those estimates from being added twice. Confirm a consistent basis and future earnings assumptions before comparing estimates.

Retained balances receive a monthly rate of (1 + annual nominal return)^(1/12) − 1, with deposits at period end. Gross benefits are historical receipts, spent benefits do not remain as wealth, and 0%-interest retained cash is an alternative to the investment balance. No starting portfolio, bridge withdrawals or other assets are modeled. Reference purchasing power divides all running totals by the common index at the observation age. This treats COLA as inflation, with effective real annual return (1 + nominal)/(1 + inflation) − 1. Historical spending is not a remaining asset.

Break-even is the first modeled monthly observation where the later claimant moves strictly ahead after previously falling behind. Equal ages have no waiting break-even; identical scenarios are labeled separately. “No crossover by 100” makes no claim about later ages. COLA, returns and longevity are assumptions, not promised outcomes. Taxes, Medicare premiums, earnings-test withholding, changes to highest-35-year earnings, disability, spousal/survivor benefits, household optimization and future law changes are excluded. No claim is made that a specific COLA, premium, average benefit or life expectancy will apply.
