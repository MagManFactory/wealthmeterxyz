Five rungs of US household wealth, from the bottom 50% to the top 0.1%. Scroll to climb. Based on Federal Reserve estimates through 2026 Q2.

How to read the ladder

US households own $185.65 trillion in total net worth.

Net worth means everything a household owns minus everything it owes. The Fed sorts all 135,198,394 households into five groups by net worth. You start at the bottom rung and climb. On each rung you will see the group total, its share, the average per household, and how it changed since the end of 2019.

These are group averages, not medians or entry thresholds. All amounts are nominal U.S. dollars. Changes since 2019 include inflation and changes in household counts.

01

Rung 1 of 5 · Bottom 50% of households

The bottom half of households holds 2.30% of the wealth.

Start at the bottom. 67.6 million households share $4.28 trillion. Debts here equal 58.4% of what these households own, and 46.9% of their assets is real estate.

Total net worth, 2026 Q2 $4.28 trillion
Share of all household wealth 2.30% was 1.73% at the end of 2019
Average per household $63,269 was $28,933 at the end of 2019
Growth since end of 2019 2.29x 1.86 to 4.28 trillion dollars

Since the end of 2019 this rung grew by the largest multiple on the ladder, but from a very small base. It added 2.41 trillion dollars. The top 0.1% added 14.51 trillion dollars.

There is no entry point for this rung. It is the bottom of the ladder.

02

Rung 2 of 5 · 50th to 90th percentile

The 50th to 90th percentile holds 28.78%.

Now the middle of the ladder: 54.1 million households. Their biggest asset is real estate at 37.3% of assets, while stocks and mutual funds are only 11.8%.

Total net worth, 2026 Q2 $53.43 trillion
Share of all household wealth 28.78% was 28.37% at the end of 2019
Average per household $988,181 was $592,464 at the end of 2019
Growth since end of 2019 1.75x 30.54 to 53.43 trillion dollars

Since the end of 2019 this rung added 22.89 trillion dollars. All households together went from 107.65 to 185.65 trillion dollars, a multiple of 1.72x.

Lowest net worth in this group in 2022 Q3, the last quarter the Fed file lists one: $240,508. Current entry points are not published in this file.

03

Rung 3 of 5 · 90th to 99th percentile

The 90th to 99th percentile holds 36.43%, the biggest slice of any rung.

12.2 million households. This rung holds more total wealth than any other, because it pairs a large group of households with solid average wealth.

Share of all household wealth 36.43% was 39.54% at the end of 2019

Growth since end of 2019 1.59x 42.56 to 67.63 trillion dollars

Since the end of 2019 this rung added 25.07 trillion dollars. All households together went from 107.65 to 185.65 trillion dollars, a multiple of 1.72x.

Lowest net worth in this group in 2022 Q3, the last quarter the Fed file lists one: $2,135,868. Current entry points are not published in this file.

05

Rung 5 of 5 · Top 0.1% of households

The top 0.1% holds 15.01%. That is about one household in a thousand.

The summit: 136,779 households. Stocks and mutual funds make up 57.3% of what they own, and debts are just 1.1% of assets.

Total net worth, 2026 Q2 $27.87 trillion
Share of all household wealth 15.01% was 12.41% at the end of 2019
Average per household $203,738,081 was $101,882,544 at the end of 2019
Growth since end of 2019 2.09x 13.36 to 27.87 trillion dollars

Since the end of 2019 this rung added 14.51 trillion dollars. All households together went from 107.65 to 185.65 trillion dollars, a multiple of 1.72x.

Lowest net worth in this group in 2022 Q3, the last quarter the Fed file lists one: $45,796,944. Current entry points are not published in this file.

The summit pulls away

The top 0.1% went from $13.36 trillion to $27.87 trillion.

That is 2.09x the 2019 level. Its share of all household wealth rose from 12.41% to 15.01%.

It also pulled away from the rest of the rich. In 2019 the top 0.1% held 0.69 dollars for every dollar held by the 99th to 99.9th percentile. Now it holds 0.86.

The same ladder, one household at a time

The average top 0.1% household holds 3,220 times the average bottom 50% household.

If the average bottom 50% household were one foot tall, the average top 0.1% household would be 3,220 feet tall, more than half a mile.

What the rungs are made of

Stocks and mutual funds are 57.3% of top 0.1% assets. For the 90th to 99th it is 33.4%. For the 50th to 90th it is 11.8%.

So when stock prices climb, the top rung feels it most. Stocks and mutual funds owned directly by the top 0.1% rose by 9.85 trillion dollars since the end of 2019, which is 67% of that group's total asset gain of 14.68 trillion dollars. The group holds 25.0% of all household stocks and mutual funds.

Source and notes

Source: Federal Reserve Distributional Financial Accounts, through 2026 Q2. Net worth by wealth percentile group, level series, compared with the end of 2019 Q4. Totals, shares, averages, multiples and asset mixes were recomputed from the published files.

Methodology caveat. These are survey-based estimates. The Fed combines the Financial Accounts totals with the Survey of Consumer Finances and fills in the quarters between and after surveys, so recent figures are estimates and may be revised. Estimates for the very top are the least certain. Groups are defined by net worth in each quarter, so they are not the same households followed over time. Lowest net worth values for each group are from 2022 Q3, the last quarter the file reports them.
