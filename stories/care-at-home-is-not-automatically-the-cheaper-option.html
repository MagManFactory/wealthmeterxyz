Care at home is not automatically the cheaper option

CareScout’s prices depend on the care setting and hours. Comparing annual totals without those assumptions can mislead a family.

Assisted living had a national median cost of $6,200 a month in CareScout's 2025 survey. Non-medical care at home had a median hourly rate of $35. The totals cannot be compared without deciding how much care is needed.

At 44 hours a week for 52 weeks, the home-care figure becomes $80,080 a year. Assisted living becomes $74,400. The home-care total does not mean every family needs 44 paid hours, while the assisted-living figure does not establish that every required service is included.

The survey collected more than 25,000 provider rates between July and November 2025. These are median provider-reported prices, not quotes for a particular household. CareScout is affiliated with Genworth, a company with a commercial interest in care planning and funding.

A family needs local quotes that specify hours, staffing, room and board, additional services and what happens if needs increase. Ten hours of support and round-the-clock assistance are different purchases.

Medicare generally does not cover long-term custodial care when that is the only care needed. Do not assume a familiar insurance card settles the funding question.

The useful comparison is the cost of arrangements that meet the same needs, alongside availability and the unpaid help family members can realistically provide. A national annual price is a starting reference, not the decision itself.
