Retirement fragility and household risk · October 6, 2026

The Health Insurance Job Lock Premium

A worker can receive a better offer and still hesitate to leave. The salary difference is visible. The cost of replacing a health plan may be harder to calculate, especially when a spouse, child, specialist, or expensive medicine depends on the current coverage. That unpriced gap is the health insurance job lock premium.

Employer coverage · household cash flow · COBRA · Marketplace timing

Job lock describes a constraint on changing work because health coverage is attached to employment. The premium is a household decision measure, not a published insurance price. Calculate it from the difference between the current plan and the feasible replacement, including premiums, out-of-pocket exposure, provider access, and the timing of coverage. A high salary or large investment account does not remove a network restriction or enrollment deadline.

Compare the full cost of coverage through the transition year. The employee payroll deduction alone does not show what the household will pay after leaving.

Put each coverage path on the same calendar

Start with the date the current plan ends and the date a new employer plan begins. Ask the prospective employer for the plan documents, dependent premiums, deductible, out-of-pocket maximum, drug formulary, and network. Verify whether a waiting period applies. A gap of even a few weeks can make a nominal pay increase less useful, especially when treatment is already scheduled.

Federal COBRA generally lets eligible workers and dependents keep group coverage temporarily after a qualifying loss. The Department of Labor says the election period is at least 60 days, measured from the later of the coverage loss or the election notice. A qualified person may be charged up to 102% of the plan's total cost, which can be much more than the former payroll deduction when the employer had paid part of the premium. COBRA preserves the group plan for a period, but the household must check the actual notice, price, payment deadline, and duration.

Marketplace access changes the calculation

HealthCare.gov says loss of qualifying job-based coverage may create a Marketplace special enrollment period if the loss occurred in the past 60 days or is expected in the next 60 days. The date and documentation matter. Voluntarily dropping COBRA before it expires generally does not create a new special enrollment period. A household should price Marketplace coverage before the old plan ends and verify the available start date.

Premium tax credits depend on household circumstances and the applicable rules. For 2026, HealthCare.gov lists 9.96% of household income as the affordability threshold for the lowest-cost qualifying employer plan. The employee test uses self-only coverage, while the family member test can use the cost to cover the household. These are eligibility rules, not a guarantee that a particular Marketplace plan will be cheaper. Quoted premiums, projected income, and expected care use must be checked for the actual household.

Measure the premium against the career decision

Build a twelve-month comparison for staying, taking the new job, and leaving employment. For each path, add premiums and a realistic range of out-of-pocket spending. Note the deductible already paid this year, the reset date on any new plan, and whether clinicians and prescriptions remain covered. Include dependent coverage and the cost of a temporary bridge. This is a scenario calculation; one claim or one premium quote cannot predict the final bill.

Then compare the coverage difference with compensation, work flexibility, retirement timing, and other benefits. A new employer may provide a lower premium but a narrower network. An independent plan may create more career freedom but require a larger cash reserve. WealthMeter's financial runway tool can test whether the household can absorb the transition, while its long-term care analysis examines a separate, later-life coverage gap.

The decision becomes clearer once the household has written quotes, plan documents, and confirmed dates. If the premium remains large after a full comparison, staying has an identifiable price. If replacement coverage is workable, the household can evaluate the job on its other merits without treating the current health plan as an immovable asset.

Sources and method

HealthCare.gov, special enrollment after coverage loss.

HealthCare.gov, 2026 employer coverage affordability rule.

U.S. Department of Labor, COBRA continuation coverage.

U.S. Department of Labor, COBRA election and premium rules.

The job lock premium is an illustrative household comparison defined in this article. It is not a survey estimate.
