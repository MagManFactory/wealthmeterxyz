Evidence and method

Real versus nominal returns

Nominal returns measure dollar growth. Real returns measure growth after the dataset’s inflation adjustment. A positive nominal return can still lose purchasing power.

By WealthMeter · Prepared with AI assistance · Sources and method reviewed 14 October 2026

The exact adjustment

For one year, real return = (1 + nominal return) / (1 + inflation) − 1. A 10% nominal return and 5% inflation therefore produce about 4.76% real growth. Subtracting inflation gives a rough approximation, not the exact calculation.

Across several years

Historical Return Windows applies this adjustment year by year before compounding. Use the nominal view for dollar balances and the real view for constant purchasing power. The replay contribution is specified in today’s purchasing-power dollars. Changing the display does not create a new source of return.

Limitations

The dataset’s broad inflation series will not match every household’s spending. Neither view is a guaranteed investment return; tax and fee effects must be considered separately.
