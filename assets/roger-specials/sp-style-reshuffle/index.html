What changes in the rulebook

S&P Dow Jones Indices announced rules that retain earnings and sales-per-share growth. They change how value is measured. Definitions and timing come from S&P DJI, linked below.

Existing rules before the effective date

Growth

  • Three-year change in earnings per share (EPS) over current price
  • Three-year annual sales-per-share growth
  • Twelve-month price change

Value

  • Book value (accounting net assets) over price
  • Trailing earnings yield
  • Sales yield

Announced rules from December 21

Growth

  • Three-year change in earnings per share (EPS) over current price
  • Three-year annual sales-per-share growth

Value

  • Intangibles-adjusted book value over price
  • Average trailing and forward earnings yields
  • Sales yield
  • Free cash flow yield, subject to exclusions

How to read the example

Per-share denominators matter

For each fiscal period, sales per share equals revenue divided by shares. The annual growth rate compares those per-share values across three years. A change in shares can change this result even if total revenue stays flat.

EPS change over price uses a difference in EPS, divided by the current price. Trailing and assumed forward earnings yields are averaged equally in the new blend.

Boundaries of this calculation

Actual S&P scores require its full standardization universe and allocation procedures. This page does not average the raw measures into a score, infer an intangible-asset adjustment or classify securities.

A zero starting sales base has no compound annual growth result here. Negative EPS, book value and cash flow retain their signs. Blank, nonfinite and invalid denominators produce an unavailable result.

Cash-flow exclusions and input formulas

The published exclusions are Real Estate, Banks, Insurance, Mortgage REITs, Specialized Finance, Asset Management and Custody Banks, and Investment Banking and Brokerage. The selector uses their published GICS codes. GICS is the Global Industry Classification Standard.

  • Sales/share = revenue in millions / shares in millions.
  • Annual sales/share growth = (latest sales/share / past sales/share) raised to one third, minus one.
  • EPS-change yield = (latest fiscal-year EPS minus EPS three years earlier) / current price.
  • Book, earnings, sales and free-cash-flow yields use their per-share amounts divided by current price.
  • Past-year price change = current price / earlier price minus one.
  • Forward/trailing blend = (trailing EPS / price + assumed forward EPS / price) / two.

Trailing twelve-month amounts are separate from the fiscal-year growth periods. The exercise has no shorter-history fallback or negative-sales-base calculation. It cannot establish real index classifications.
