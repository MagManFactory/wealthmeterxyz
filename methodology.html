Methodology.

WealthMeter combines wealth benchmarks, planning calculators and dated data comparisons. Each uses a different population, source or set of assumptions. A model percentile, a historical return and a household survey band should be interpreted within their own definitions.

Methodology reviewed: October 3, 2026

0. Scope and Wealth Definition

The homepage calculator uses the UBS Global Wealth Report 2026 year-end 2025 distribution: approximately 3.846 billion adults across 56 markets representing more than 92% of world wealth. It does not extrapolate the covered-market distribution to all adults worldwide. UBS defines wealth as financial assets plus real assets, including funded private pensions, less debts. State pension entitlements and human capital are excluded. Household wealth is allocated to adults for this per-adult model.

1. Source-Anchored Rank Model

The engine estimates the number of adults at or above a net-worth value by interpolating between published population anchors. Interpolation is linear in logarithmic wealth and logarithmic population space, which avoids the distortion produced by ordinary linear interpolation across large wealth ranges.

log N(w) = log N₁ + [log(w/w₁) / log(w₂/w₁)] × [log N₂ − log N₁]

Counts published in rounded wealth bands are themselves approximate. WealthMeter preserves that limitation rather than presenting the output as a census.

2. Boundary and Upper-Tail Treatment

UBS publishes the lowest tier only as “below USD 10,000.” WealthMeter therefore returns the full published percentile and rank interval for values in that tier; it does not invent an exact position. Above USD 10,000, the model uses adjacent anchors. Above USD 100 billion, it extrapolates only the slope between the final two UBS anchors and floors the result at rank one. The former single Pareto exponent has been removed.

3. Currency, Rank, and Percentile

Currency inputs use European Central Bank reference rates dated September 15, 2026, converted to U.S. dollars per currency unit. Rank one represents the highest wealth. The conventional percentile is calculated as 100 × (1 − rank ÷ 3.846 billion). The “top share” is rank ÷ 3.846 billion. Country cards scale that share to an approximate adult headcount for perspective; they are not country-specific wealth ranks.

4. U.S. Household Wealth Calculator

The standalone U.S. household calculator uses the Census Bureau's 2025 Survey of Income and Program Participation wealth tables for calendar year 2024, released July 15, 2026. Below USD 500,000, WealthMeter calculates cumulative percentages and rank intervals directly from the nine published Table 4 bands and performs no within-band interpolation.

Table 4 ends with an open USD 500,000-or-more category. WealthMeter divides that category using the weighted upper-tail shape of the Federal Reserve's 2022 Survey of Consumer Finances summary extract. The calculation uses all five implicates and the SCF analysis weight, converts net worth from 2022 to 2024 dollars with CPI-U annual averages, and normalizes the resulting survival curve at USD 500,000. For age, region, and housing-tenure comparisons, the selected group's published Census share at USD 500,000 or more controls the size of the tail; the national SCF curve controls its internal shape.

Group survival above w = Census group share above $500K × [SCF survival above w ÷ SCF survival above $500K]

The hybrid bands are USD 500,000–999,999; USD 1–2.49 million; USD 2.5–4.99 million; USD 5–9.99 million; USD 10–24.99 million; USD 25–49.99 million; USD 50–99.99 million; USD 100–249.99 million; USD 250–499.99 million; USD 500–999.99 million; and USD 1 billion or more. These are modeled intervals, not published Census ranks. The Census household and SCF primary economic unit definitions are similar but not identical; normalization at USD 500,000 limits rather than eliminates that mismatch. The calculator reports point-estimate bands and does not calculate replicate-weight confidence intervals. Sampling uncertainty and public-data limitations become material above USD 100 million. This household output should not be compared directly with the UBS per-adult result.

5. Sources and Limitations

Primary references are the UBS Global Wealth Report 2026, Knight Frank Wealth Sizing Model 2026, and ECB reference rates. The U.S. household calculator specifically uses the Census Bureau 2024 wealth tables, the official detailed workbook, SIPP methodology, the Federal Reserve's 2022 SCF documentation and public summary extract, and BLS CPI-U annual averages. Exchange rates change daily. Survey estimates, modeled counts, rounded bands, asset valuation dates, and differences between household and individual definitions all limit precision. Results are informational and are not financial advice.

6. Global Wealth Data Lab

The Global Wealth Data Lab presents a static, dated snapshot rather than live API responses. Its 50-country comparison set uses the latest observation available in the snapshot for each displayed measure. Observation years remain visible because publication schedules differ across providers; the interface does not imply that unlike years are simultaneous.

Country profiles and comparisons use direct observations from the World Development Indicators, World Inequality Database, and U.S. Census Bureau International Database. Bars show relative magnitude within one selected measure only. They are not a composite score or global rank. Derived findings combine only records with compatible units and retain their source identifiers. A former consumption-to-GDP ratio was removed because its numerator and denominator used incompatible price bases.

OECD Economic Outlook 107 observations describe the 2020-Q2 and 2021-Q4 pandemic outlook. They are segregated in a historical archive and excluded from the current comparison and “Surprise me” modes. The versioned source and definition register is displayed inside the Data Lab. This product uses the Census Bureau Data API but is not endorsed or certified by the Census Bureau.

7. Quality-Adjusted Purchasing Power

Live Better for Less combines national private-consumption price levels with public systems indicators. The price-level index is the World Bank private-consumption PPP conversion factor divided by the official exchange rate, with the United States set to 100. A destination budget is calculated as home monthly spending multiplied by the destination price level divided by the home price level. This estimates a comparable national consumption basket. It does not increase financial assets or establish personal equivalence.

The quality screen uses six measures: life expectancy, electricity access, internet use, safely managed sanitation, trade and transport infrastructure quality, and intentional homicide. A candidate needs at least five observations and must include life expectancy, electricity, internet, and sanitation. No missing value is imputed. The selected quality-floor preset sets the maximum permitted shortfall for each measure; safety uses a ceiling relative to the home-country homicide rate. User-selected priorities receive additional weight, but nonpriority measures retain hard floors.

The “Quality-screened options” mode in Lifestyle Abroad applies the same close-match hard floors before producing any shortlist. It gives the six available measures equal weight and uses the same 72% quality-preservation and 28% savings balance. The direct home-to-destination mode remains a price translation chosen by the user; it does not imply that the destination passed the quality screen.

Destination monthly estimate = home monthly spending × (destination private-consumption price level ÷ home private-consumption price level)
Quality preservation = weighted mean of measure-level preservation scores after all hard floors pass

Results are ordered by a disclosed balance of quality preservation (72%) and estimated spending reduction (28%). This is not a global quality-of-life ranking. It is a constrained comparison against the selected home country. National averages do not represent a particular city, neighborhood, household, expatriate basket, or legal relocation pathway. Visas, taxes, housing tenure, healthcare eligibility, language, climate, political conditions, imported-goods exposure, and exchange-rate changes are not modeled.

Price sources are World Bank indicators PA.NUS.PRVT.PP and PA.NUS.FCRF. Quality measures are delivered through World Development Indicators, with the underlying source organizations identified in the interface. Each result exposes its observation year. World Bank PPP guidance treats these values as statistical approximations and cautions against strict rankings based on small differences.

Calculator and tool directory

The global and U.S. household methods above remain distinct. Other tools use the methods below. Publication and retrieval dates belong to the underlying datasets; this page’s review date does not make older observations current.

Global Wealth Rank

Inputs: Covered-market per-adult rank; see sections 0–3.

Method and limits: Published wealth anchors, currency conversion and log-space interpolation. Coverage excludes adults outside the included markets.

U.S. Household Wealth

Inputs: Household net worth with optional demographic comparison; see section 4.

Method and limits: Published Census bands below $500,000 and a normalized SCF upper-tail model. Household and per-adult results are not interchangeable.

Country-Specific Rank

Inputs: Net worth in USD and a selected country.

Method and limits: Piecewise interpolation between embedded median, top-10% and top-1% thresholds, with a capped upper-tail extension and adult headcounts. This separate simplified model is not the homepage UBS interpolation; it does not establish an exact census rank.

Income Percentile

Inputs: Income, currency, country, age and sex.

Method and limits: Age-band medians and an embedded gender adjustment define a reference income. Piecewise interpolation across stored percentile ratios supplies an approximate position. Source dates vary, typically 2019–2023; the demographic adjustments do not constitute complete sex-by-age survey distributions.

Net Worth Calibration

Inputs: User-entered assets and liabilities.

Method and limits: Sum assets and subtract debts. Asset valuations and ownership shares are supplied by the user; no valuation or creditor verification is performed.

FIRE Timeline Planner

Inputs: Net worth, annual saving, expenses, withdrawal-rate assumption, real return and age.

Method and limits: Target capital derives from annual expenses divided by the assumed withdrawal rate. Constant real returns and annual saving determine the accumulation path. No withdrawal rule is guaranteed safe.

FIRE & Cost of Living

Inputs: Spending and location assumptions.

Method and limits: Translate spending using the tool’s location factors and inspect the implied FIRE target. This older assumption-based comparison differs from the national PPP and quality-screen model in section 7.

Portfolio Alpha Simulator

Inputs: Starting capital, annual additions, years, return, inflation and effective tax assumption.

Method and limits: Add each annual contribution before compounding, apply the entered tax rate to positive modeled gains at the end, then divide by cumulative inflation. The model does not represent account-specific tax rules or investment alpha measured against a benchmark.

Financial Runway Lab

Inputs: Capital, annual spending, yield and baseline/target location factors.

Method and limits: Apply constant yield and subtract annual spending until depletion, capped at 100 simulated years; target spending scales by target factor divided by baseline factor. Built-in location modifiers are assumptions, not a validated personal relocation budget.

Wealth Explorer

Inputs: Country selection and sort order.

Method and limits: Combine official UBS 2026 medians where available with explicitly labeled 2025-harmonized estimates for other markets and upper thresholds. The mixed-vintage display is not a complete 2026 distribution.

Live Better for Less

Inputs: Home spending, country and quality preferences.

Method and limits: Translate private-consumption price levels and apply indicator-specific quality floors; see section 7. National averages exclude visas, household taxes and city-specific costs.

Lifestyle Abroad

Inputs: Home and destination country, spending and optional quality screen.

Method and limits: Direct mode translates a chosen national consumption basket. Screened mode applies the shared quality floors described above; selecting a destination alone does not establish eligibility or quality preservation.

Country Systems Atlas

Inputs: Two or three countries and a selected measure.

Method and limits: Compare eight World Bank measures in the complete 145-economy set. Each year remains visible; bars have no cross-measure or overall-ranking interpretation.

Longevity Atlas

Inputs: Country and sort order.

Method and limits: Display country lifespan baselines alongside assumed LEV planning bands and wealth context. Extended horizons are speculative scenarios, not clinical forecasts.

Data Lab

Inputs: Country, measure and comparison selection.

Method and limits: Use dated compatible-unit snapshots from WDI, WID and Census; segregate historical OECD outlooks. See section 6 and the in-tool source register.

The Long Ladder

Inputs: Quarter and wealth-group comparisons.

Method and limits: Recompute Fed Distributional Financial Accounts shares, averages, multiples and asset mixes. See the dedicated method below; group membership can change between quarters.

Wealth Briefs

Inputs: Topic, year, source and text filters.

Method and limits: Search the existing research-summary library. Each brief’s original source, date and scope govern interpretation; a brief is not a calculator or an individualized recommendation.

Wealth Horizon

Wealth Horizon accepts a starting portfolio, annual contributions, a 5-to-50-year horizon, return, volatility, inflation, fees, estimated tax drag and contribution growth. Contributions enter monthly and increase once a year. The deterministic path compounds the assumed return after fees and tax drag; the real value divides the final balance by cumulative inflation.

The range uses 2,000 reproducible simulations of independent monthly lognormal returns with a fixed seed. Lower and higher results are the 10th and 90th percentiles. They are model outcomes under the selected assumptions; outcomes outside that range remain possible.

Presets are editable illustrations. The model imports no historical return forecast and excludes withdrawals, changing allocations, irregular cash flows and persistent market regimes. Tax drag is an annual return reduction, not an account-specific tax calculation. The tool retains compound-interest, inflation and fee references in its source section.

Historical Return Windows

Historical Return Windows uses Damodaran’s NYU Stern annual U.S. stock total returns, 10-year Treasury bond returns, average 3-month Treasury bill rates and CPI inflation for 1928–2025, retrieved October 1, 2026. Every complete start-year window is compounded. Annualized growth is the total growth factor raised to 1 divided by years, minus 1. Real growth compounds (1 + nominal return) divided by (1 + inflation).

Historical replays interpret starting capital and contributions in constant purchasing-power dollars. Twelve monthly contributions are approximated as one inflation-indexed annual contribution arriving at midyear. Portfolios rebalance annually. Taxes, fees, withdrawals and allocation changes are excluded. Overlapping windows are not independent; the U.S. record and annual observations limit generalization. Historical frequencies do not establish future probabilities.

The Long Ladder

The Long Ladder uses Federal Reserve Distributional Financial Accounts through 2026 Q2, with growth comparisons from 2019 Q4. Wealth shares divide each group’s net worth by the all-household total; average household wealth divides group net worth by its household count. Asset mixes divide each component by group total assets. Dollars are nominal and growth is not inflation-adjusted.

The Fed combines Financial Accounts totals with Survey of Consumer Finances estimates and models intervening quarters. Revisions and upper-tail uncertainty remain. Percentile groups are not fixed households followed over time. The source supplies no current group entry cutoffs; the latest displayed cutoffs are dated 2022 Q3. These groups cannot be used as current personal thresholds or equated with the separate Census household calculator.

Country Systems Atlas

Country Systems Atlas compares 145 countries and economies with complete coverage across eight World Development Indicators measures: GDP per person at purchasing-power parity, life expectancy, electricity access, urban population, transport infrastructure, logistics performance, manufacturing share and capital formation.

Each observation retains its year and indicator source. Bars scale one measure within the included comparison set. They cannot be compared across rows and do not constitute a composite development score or a global population rank. Investment and settlement shares are descriptive; larger values are not automatically better. The source snapshot and reuse register remain available inside the tool.

Dates, assumptions and access

A linked tool’s source register provides its measurement definition, observation year and build context. Older embedded threshold and location models can lag newer source-backed features. Their outputs should be treated as approximations until their own calibration is reviewed. Subscriber gates change access, not the calculation or the strength of evidence.

Changes to a tool’s dataset, formula, contribution timing or reported population require a corresponding methodology update.
