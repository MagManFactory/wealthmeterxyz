Decision-grade wealth evidence, without longform sprawl

Wealth Briefs is a compact reference layer for recurring financial claims. Each brief separates empirical support from implementation judgment, shows where the tactic works, and marks where the evidence is mixed, regime-dependent, or easily overstated.

What this section is for

Longform explains systems and tradeoffs across a full narrative arc. Wealth Briefs does something narrower. It compresses a single strategy or claim into a repeatable evidence card so the user can compare tactics on the same frame: support level, mechanism, boundaries, friction, and action stance.

Established fact Empirical baseline fields are reserved for what the cited source class actually supports.
Reasoned inference Action stance reflects implementation judgment, not a disguised citation.
Source hierarchy Peer-reviewed research, official datasets, and institutional scorecards outrank books or opinion-led advice.

Evidence policy

Grade A Repeated support across long samples, multiple studies, or official scorecards. Good default decision material.
Grade B Useful but conditional. Often tax-sensitive, regime-sensitive, or highly dependent on household structure.
Grade C Plausible or early, but not strong enough for confident default advice.
Books and reports Consumer books can frame an idea, but they do not automatically elevate it to decision-grade evidence.
