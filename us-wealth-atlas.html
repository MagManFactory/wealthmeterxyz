How should I read these numbers?

Distributional Financial Accounts figures are aggregate balance-sheet estimates. A group can hold a large share because it contains many families, because its typical family is wealthier, or both. The asset view shows who owns each category across the country. It does not say what percentage of a group’s own portfolio is invested in that asset.

Why do the age views differ?

The DFA view divides all aggregate net worth among four age groups. The SCF view reports the median family inside each age band. Aggregate shares reflect both family counts and the size of very large fortunes; medians describe the family in the middle of each band.

Sources and method

Distribution and balance-sheet figures come from the Federal Reserve Distributional Financial Accounts, through 2026 Q2. Age-band medians come from the Federal Reserve 2025 Survey of Consumer Finances report and are expressed in 2025 dollars. Annual history uses each year’s Q2 observation. The top 1% combines the published top 0.1% and remaining top 1% series. Figures may not sum to exactly 100 because of rounding.

Caveats

The DFA and SCF use related but different methods. These figures describe groups, not individual households. They do not measure liquidity, local living costs, taxes, or whether a particular balance sheet is resilient. The page does not estimate inheritance or forecast future transfers.
