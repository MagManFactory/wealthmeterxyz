America Is 2nd by Average Wealth, 15th by Median Wealth

UBS’s June 30, 2026 Global Wealth Report places the United States second by average wealth per adult and fifteenth by median wealth per adult. Those are not competing answers. They describe different parts of the same distribution.

September 1, 2026 · 8 min read

Average wealth adds all assets and debts, then divides by adults. Median wealth marks the person at the middle of the distribution. A country with a large stock market, valuable businesses, and concentrated ownership can rank very highly by the first measure while ranking lower by the second. The distinction matters because households do not finance a deductible, a job loss, or retirement spending from national averages.

The mean measures the scale of a national balance sheet. The median is closer to the margin available to a household in the middle.

The dated source resolves the rank question

UBS’s 2026 table reports average wealth per adult of USD 696,277 for the United States, behind Switzerland and ahead of Luxembourg. In its median-wealth column, the United States is listed fifteenth at USD 68,998. A rank without a year, definition, and source is not a durable fact. This table supplies all three.

Why households should not use either rank as a plan

The Federal Reserve’s 2022 Survey of Consumer Finances provides the domestic mechanism: the top 10 percent of families held 69.6 percent of net worth, while the bottom half held 2.5 percent. International rankings cannot reveal a household’s liquidity, debt service, insurance exposure, ownership of productive assets, or time-to-cash. Those determine whether a balance sheet survives an interruption.

The free WealthMeter Explorer is the appropriate next step. Use a comparison as context, then inspect cash reserves, high-cost debt, asset concentration, and the obligations that arrive before an asset can be sold. A favorable national ranking does not create household optionality.
