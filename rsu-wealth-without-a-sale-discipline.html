LONGFORM · PRIVATE BALANCE-SHEET TRAPS

RSU Wealth Without a Sale Discipline

OCTOBER 20, 2026 · WEALTHMETER

An employee sees a large restricted stock unit balance in a compensation portal. After a vesting event, shares arrive in a brokerage account. The household now owns a security whose value can move sharply, while the tax and spending plans may still assume that the award has become dependable wealth.

Restricted stock units (RSUs) are promises to deliver stock or cash when plan conditions are met. The grant value displayed in a portal can change before delivery and cannot fund current spending. Vesting and settlement terms control when shares are delivered. FINRA notes that employee awards may vest over time and that restrictions vary. The award agreement and payroll records are the starting documents.

Vesting and selling are separate events

For a typical stock-settled public-company RSU, share delivery after vesting creates compensation income reported through payroll. The IRS has described stock delivered in settlement of an RSU as wages on Form W-2. The exact income date can depend on the plan’s vesting and payment terms. A later sale is a separate stock disposition. The gain or loss generally reflects the difference between sale proceeds and the stock’s applicable tax basis, after the compensation value has been accounted for.

Confusing these events can lead an employee to treat a sale as if the full proceeds were newly taxable wages, or to assume that income tax waits until the shares are sold. Payroll withholding and final tax liability can differ. A broker statement may also need to be reconciled with W-2 income and basis records. The IRS Publication 525 describes special section 83(i) rules for qualifying private-company awards; those rules should not be applied automatically to ordinary public-company RSUs.

The employer risk sits in two places

Salary, future awards, and a large stock position can all depend on the same company. If its business weakens, job security and portfolio value may fall together. FINRA calls a large share of assets in one stock concentration risk and advises employees to consider all employer-stock holdings, including those in retirement accounts. The SEC explains that diversification spreads exposure across investments, though it cannot eliminate market risk.

A rising share price can make a plan to sell feel premature. Yet each new vest can add more of the same security. Without a rule, the household may accumulate employer stock through default inaction. A sale discipline sets a process before price swings and emotions decide. It need not prescribe a universal percentage or force every share to be sold immediately. The appropriate exposure depends on household reserves, other assets, tax situation, plan restrictions, and risk capacity.

Write the rule before the next vest

List outstanding awards by vesting date and conditions, then separate unvested promises from shares already owned. Record the value included in wages at each delivery and reconcile it with payroll and brokerage records. Compare employer shares with total investable assets and note whether employment income and retirement plans add to the same exposure.

Define the decision point: which shares are eligible for sale, when a trading window opens, and what exposure would trigger a reduction. Employees subject to company trading policies, blackout periods, or material nonpublic information restrictions need to follow those rules. A tax adviser can check basis, withholding, capital gain or loss, and unusual plan terms before a transaction. An investment adviser can test the exposure against the household’s full asset allocation.

What the balance sheet should count

Unvested RSUs can be useful for forecasting possible compensation, but they remain conditional. Delivered shares have a current market value and a tax basis. Cash from a completed sale is available for reserves, debt reduction, or diversified investment after taxes and costs. These categories serve different household decisions, so a single portal total is a poor substitute for them.

RSUs can create substantial wealth. The durable result depends on what the household does after delivery: verify the tax record, choose a permitted sale process, and limit a concentration that its wider balance sheet cannot absorb.
