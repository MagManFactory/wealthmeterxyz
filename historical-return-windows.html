When time
beat risk.

Stocks lost money in many single years. Across the full U.S. record from 1928 through 2025, longer holding periods changed the range of outcomes. Explore every available window, then replay your own horizon.

Historical U.S. evidence, not a forecast. The record reflects one unusually successful market and overlapping periods that are not independent observations.

01 · The odds changed with time

One year was noisy. Longer windows narrowed the historical range.

Each row uses every possible start year with a complete holding period. “Real” removes inflation from the compounded return.

02 · Every historical window

The red windows faded as the holding period lengthened.

Each block is a complete S&P 500 return window. Choose nominal returns or inflation-adjusted returns, then inspect a holding period and start year.

Rows show holding periods from 1 to 30 years. Columns show start years. Red indicates an annualized loss; blue indicates a gain. Color intensity is capped at 12% per year so extreme single-year results do not dominate the scale.

The difference between fortunate and difficult starting dates became smaller.

The band shows the worst and best annualized real stock return for every holding period. The line shows the median.

Source and method

Definitions before interpretation.

Source

Annual S&P 500 total returns, 10-year Treasury bond returns, average 3-month Treasury bill rates, and CPI inflation for 1928–2025 come from Aswath Damodaran at NYU Stern. Review the source and workbook. Data retrieved October 1, 2026.

Return calculations

Window returns compound the annual observations. Annualized return equals the compounded growth factor raised to 1 divided by the number of years, minus 1. Real returns compound (1 + nominal return) divided by (1 + inflation) for each year.

Calculator assumptions

The starting balance and monthly contribution are interpreted in purchasing-power dollars at the beginning of each historical window. Contributions increase with the source inflation series so their real value remains constant. Twelve monthly contributions are approximated as one annual amount added at midyear. The portfolio is rebalanced annually.

Limits

Past performance does not predict future results. This is a U.S.-only historical record with survivorship and country-selection bias. Windows overlap and are not independent. Annual observations conceal the order of returns within each year. The model excludes taxes, fees, withdrawals, and behavior changes.

Not financial advice. This feature provides general educational information and does not recommend any investment, allocation, or financial action.
